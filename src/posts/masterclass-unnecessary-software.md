hello world!

that's probably the sixth-quintillion time you've read that if you're familiar with the world of software engineering (I'd hope so if you're on this website!). Anyway, as I'm sure you can tell from the title, this blog post will cover why it was necessary to create this brand-new portfolio site rather than salvaging my original portfolio site, Portfolio95.

I'd like to begin by saying it wasn't an easy decision (I mean, how much more official does a portfolio site get than when it gets a whole LinkedIn post dedicated to it?). There were a lot of things I was planning to add to Portfolio95 (talk about scope creep), and I was set to do some more work on the site this summer. One day, while scrolling Instagram reels, I came across a tech influencer, got curious, and looked at their portfolio site. I suppose something in me thought "wow, this is a pretty neat website!", while something else said "my housemate sure has a dope portfolio site too" (you know, the problem everyone has at university: living with a cracked engineer who just so happens to be on the same university course as you, shoutout to [April](https://itisapril.dev/)). 

Once you combine those factors, you can see why anyone in my shoes would jump at the chance to spend their weekend in the middle of August, right after the fifth-hottest day on record in the UK, indoors, fighting React errors and trying to make a cool website.

The whole philosophy behind this new portfolio site is to keep it as simple as possible, both for me as the sole developer and for whatever cool person visits it next. The old site was fun to build, but it over time it became a lot to maintain let alone build upon, and I wanted something that felt more accessible to someone who isn't necessarily technical, without losing the personality that made the old site fun. So this one's typography-led rather than a full desktop simulation, with a few smaller touches instead.

I'll keep working on a multitude of easter eggs and other little hidden bits to make it more personalised, and hopefully impress some of the more senior engineers on my team during my upcoming grad role.

In the meantime, if you'd like to reach out, feel free to connect with me over on LinkedIn or shoot me an email, if it's any technical talk, I'd love to chat!
